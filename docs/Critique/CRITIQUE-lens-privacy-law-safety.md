# Critique lens: privacy, law, trust and safety (all modules)

Date: 2026-10-06. Method: read the code and docs (identity service, governance migration 005, Vyapar/Counsel privacy code, Mangalya safety and trust code, Milavn safety routes, `docs/ParentApp/08-security-performance.md`, Mangalya `08-security-performance.md` and `v1-decisions.md` DEC-V1-010), then looked at the live screens at 390px as Asha in my own browser context (read-only, nothing submitted). Law and news evidence was gathered by a research sub-agent; the shared web-search budget ran out part way, so items marked **[U]** are unverified or rest on one secondary source. I am not a lawyer; this is an engineering-and-product critique, not legal advice.

## 1. My understanding (please correct me)

ForKhatri is one app with one sign-in (phone or email code, password optional) and a thin platform identity: name, phone/email, language, locality. Everything sensitive lives in module databases behind row-level security: Mangalya (matrimony, family circles, horoscope, face-match badge), Milavn (events, circles, women-only groups), Vyapar (jobs and businesses), Counsel (paid one-to-one advice). Samaj office bearers run the app through an admin console with scoped roles, time-boxed terms, two-person approval for bans and role grants, and an append-only audit log. A Welfare Trust inside the planned Finance module receives earnings. The team has taken privacy more seriously than most startups at this stage: Vyapar has a real privacy page, export and anonymising delete; Counsel has export, erase, file encryption and retention; Mangalya has a written safety-tier model with response deadlines and a retention decision grounded in the DPDP Rules. The weakness is that this care is **per module and uneven**, and the **platform layer that law treats as the data fiduciary has almost none of it**.

Timing matters. The DPDP Rules 2025 were notified 13-14 Nov 2025. Rules 1, 2 and 17-21 (including the Data Protection Board) apply now; consent-manager Rule 4 applies about Nov 2026; Rules 3 and 5-16 (notice, security, breach, children, rights, duties) apply about mid-May 2027, roughly seven months from today. MeitY floated cutting 18 months to 12 for large players; I found no notified change **[U]**. Sources: PIB backgrounder, 17 Nov 2025, https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf ; Rules text https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf .

## 2. Critiques, ranked

### Critical

**C1. No consent notice, terms, privacy link or age question at sign-up.**
Evidence (screen, 2026-10-06): the entrance asks only "mobile number or email", then a name. The only privacy line is "Your number is never shown to other members." The account sheet has no privacy, terms or help link. The identity schema has no consent or notice-version table (`001-initial.sql`, `005-governance.sql`). Only Vyapar has an acceptance gate (`require_accepted`, versioned legal documents). The DPDP Rules require a separate, plain-language notice stating the specific purpose; PIB says so (17 Nov 2025). Penalty tiers: up to Rs 250 crore for weak security safeguards, Rs 200 crore each for missing breach notification and for children's-data failures (PIB, same source).
Why it matters: the platform holds the phone number, the one identifier that links a person's matrimonial, advice and job activity. It is the fiduciary in the most literal sense.
Recommendation: a versioned notice and terms at first sign-in in all three languages (what is collected, per module purpose, who at the samaj can see it, how to withdraw), a `consent_record` table (member, notice version, language, time), and a re-prompt when the version changes. Per-module consent screens then link up to it. Effort M.

**C2. Whole-account deletion and export do not exist; module behaviour is inconsistent.**
Evidence: `identity_app` has no DELETE on members and no endpoint deletes or exports a member (`members.py`, `auth.py`); `09-implementation.md` lists "cross-module account deletion" as an open gap; Counsel's backlog T1 says the same and its screen says "Your ForKhatri account is not affected." On screen: Vyapar Privacy has "Download my data" and "Delete my account"; Counsel `/me` has "Download my data" and "Delete my Counsel data"; the Mangalya Settings sheet (language, appearance, text size, create-for-child, log out) and the Milavn `/me` page have neither. Mangalya's DEC-V1-010 sets 30-day grace, 90-day erasure and a 48-hour pre-erasure notice, and the settings exist (`retention_post_deletion_*`), but I found no code that reads them or any delete route. Vyapar's delete says "Your name is removed everywhere", which is untrue of the ForKhatri identity and other modules.
Why it matters: erasure, access and correction are statutory rights with a 90-day response window (PIB, 17 Nov 2025). Mangalya holds the most intimate data (photos, horoscope, family, chats) and has the least self-service control. A woman who wants her matrimonial profile gone after a bad experience cannot do it herself today.
Recommendation: one "Your data" sheet on the platform account that calls each module's export/erase/deletion-check (Counsel already exposes these for the platform) and shows per-module status; build Mangalya and Milavn endpoints first; fix Vyapar's wording; set `status='deleted'` and null the phone/email in identity after all modules confirm. Effort L.

**C3. No age gate outside Mangalya, and DPDP has no youth exemption.**
Evidence: identity stores no date of birth; sign-up cannot refuse a 16-year-old. Mangalya enforces marriageable age from DOB (21 male, 18 female per `thresholds.py`) and has an operator sweep for under-18 profiles; Milavn, Vyapar and Counsel have no age field in the code I read. The Act defines a child as under 18, requires verifiable parental consent, and bans tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children (Act s.9, https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf). The Rule 12 / Fourth Schedule exemptions cover clinics, schools, creches, child transport, email-only accounts, real-time safety and similar; none fits a community app. Vyapar's "posts picked for you" taste learning and Milavn's interest-based discovery are exactly the behavioural profiling the child clause targets.
Recommendation: decide now. The cheapest defensible rule is "ForKhatri is for 18 and over", with a declared date of birth at first sign-in, an under-18 stop screen, and a Mangalya-style sweep when a minor is reported. If youth are wanted (the Khatri youth meetups are a natural pitch), plan verifiable parental consent for that cohort and switch off personalisation for it. Effort S for 18+, L for parental consent.

**C4. The Mangalya "photos confirmed as you" badge can be forged, and the profile-maker gate relies on it.**
Evidence: the code comment in `components/trust/interface.py` states that camera, liveness and face comparison run in the browser and anyone can post `distance = 0.1, liveness_passed = true`; the review queue "does not yet" exist. The same check is the gate that allows a parent to create an adult child's profile (DEC-V1-025).
Why it matters: matrimonial fraud is the dominant harm pattern. I4C's 27 Nov 2025 advisory describes fake profiles with stolen photos posing as NRIs and professionals, leading to investment schemes (https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/i4c-detects-two-latest-cyber-crime-trends-in-india-enn25112703639); an MHA/I4C matrimonial advisory (8 Jul 2026) lists fast-to-marry and refusing video calls as red flags (https://www.aajtak.in/india/news/story/matrimonial-fraud-mha-i4c-fake-profile-cyber-crime-online-marriage-scam-ntc-agkp-rptc-2596752-2026-07-08). A forgeable badge is worse than none, because families will rely on it. Courts have weighed exactly this: Allahabad HC (26 Sep 2025) quashed a case against Shaadi.com's founder because it showed due diligence (https://storyboard18.com/brand-makers/allahabad-hc-exempts-shaadi-com-ceo-from-liability-over-user-actions-81790.htm), while Telangana HC (25 Apr 2025) let an FIR stand until the Supreme Court stayed it on 26 Jun 2025 (https://inc42.com/buzz/sc-stays-cheating-case-against-shaadi-coms-anupam-mittal). Documented, honest verification helps in court; a claim that cannot be backed does not.
Recommendation: until server-checkable, relabel the badge honestly ("selfie check done on this phone"), do not use it as a gate for acting on someone else's behalf, and add a second route that is hard to fake in a community: a short video call with a local samaj verifier who knows the family, plus a signed on-device attestation later. DEC-V1-016's refusal to store faces is right and compatible with this. Effort M.

### High

**H1. No accountable data fiduciary, grievance officer or breach plan is visible to members, and the legal entity behind "samaj runs the app" is undefined.**
Evidence: `admin-console.md` lists "grievance officer, data protection officer" as platform staff roles, but no member-facing page, contact or SLA exists on the platform; only Vyapar shows grievance text (ReportSheet, outcomes page). I found no breach-response document in `docs/ParentApp` or the module Step-8 files. Rule 7 requires telling each affected person without delay and giving the Board a detailed report within 72 hours; CERT-In (28 Apr 2022) requires reporting listed incidents within 6 hours and keeping logs 180 days in India (https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf). Under IT Act s.85, people "in charge of and responsible to" a company, defined to include associations of individuals, can be personally liable unless they show no knowledge or due diligence (https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1708482/). Volunteer office bearers are therefore exposed, not shielded.
Recommendation: incorporate or formally register the operating body (a Section 8 company or registered society) before launch, separate from the Welfare Trust; name a grievance officer and a data-protection contact on a platform "Help and legal" page; write a one-page breach runbook with an on-call owner and templates in three languages; obtain directors-and-officers cover. Effort M.

**H2. Admin access to deeply personal data by neighbours, with real controls but gaps.**
Good: roles are code, terms end automatically (`ends_at`), bans and role grants need a second person, revealing a phone or email is audited, the audit table rejects UPDATE/DELETE by trigger. Gaps: no conflict-of-interest rule (a city-chapter head moderating a report about a relative or business rival); no member-visible "who looked at my data" record; the audit trigger can be dropped by the owner and there is no hash chain or external copy; the cross-module "member summaries" view (`/admin/members/{id}/modules`) is scoped by role, but I could not confirm that reads are audited; no confidentiality undertaking, training or term-end access review is recorded.
Recommendation: recusal on declared relationship, mandatory reason on every sensitive view, a monthly "access report" the president must countersign, a signed undertaking at role grant, and ship the audit log to write-once storage. Effort M.

**H3. Women's safety controls exist but rest on self-declaration and English-only signals.**
Evidence: Mangalya has block, report (harassment, fake profile, scam, underage, already married), tiered response times (high tier 12h, legal subset 2h, paging) and regex detection of money asks, UPI ids and phone numbers in chat (`detection.py`; patterns are English and Latin script). Milavn's QA run of 2026-10-06 found women-only groups rely on a self-declared answer: a man who has not joined anything can switch his answer to woman and enter, and a man can be invited to a women-only circle (error only on accept). Milavn's profile page lists upcoming events with "~2.4 km away" next to host names, and Mangalya's own Step-8 document admits that on iOS screenshots of messages can only be detected, not prevented, and that no OS can stop a second phone photographing the screen.
Context: a Juleo/YouGov survey reported 78% of Indian women on dating/matrimony apps met fake profiles (Feminism in India, 1 Dec 2025, https://feminisminindia.com/2025/12/01/from-rishta-to-risk-scams-and-ai-morphing-fueling-gendered-violence-on-indian-matrimonial-sites/).
Recommendation: women-only groups should be gated by an organiser or moderator approving each joiner (the join-question feature in migration 024 is the right base), not a checkbox; extend scam detection to Hindi, Telugu and romanised forms; add in-chat banners ("never send money; call 1930"); give women an "only show my photo after a request" default; add StopNCII-style hash blocking for intimate-image reports (hash made on the device, https://stopncii.org/how-it-works/); recruit women moderators for women's reports. NCW helpline 14490 and the national 1930 line should be one tap in the report sheet (https://www.ncw.gov.in/). Effort M.

**H4. Affinity fraud is the real threat, and the Khatri brand lowers people's guard.**
Evidence: I4C says 46.58 lakh portal complaints and Rs 30,983 crore financial-fraud losses over three years (ETV Bharat, 27 Nov 2025); the Perth-based man "Dr Rohit Oberoi" messaged at least 3,194 women and took Rs 3.6 crore from one (Tribune, Jun 2025, https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/diaspora/perth-based-nri-messaged-3000-women-on-matrimonial-site-using-fake-identity). In a closed community "he is one of us" substitutes for checking. Vyapar job posts (registration-fee scams), Milavn (invites to private schemes), Mangalya (NRI grooms) and the planned Finance module (deposit-like community schemes) are all exposed. The Banning of Unregulated Deposit Schemes Act 2019 carries 2-7 years' imprisonment for taking money with a promise of return; whether a samaj society is exempt is **[U]**. RBI's 2025 digital-lending directions keep the regulated lender "fully responsible" for its referral partners (https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12848&Mode=0).
Recommendation: ban "pay to apply", member-to-member money requests and any pooled community investment in product rules; show the scam warning that matches the module at the moment of contact; require a video call before contact exchange for Mangalya; launch Finance only as a referral to regulated lenders with no community deposit or investment features; and brief elders, the main victims of "digital arrest" and WhatsApp scams, in Hindi and Telugu (loss figures I saw are single-source **[U]**). Effort M.

**H5. Purpose limitation: Mangalya data feeds a platform dashboard.**
Evidence: `insights.py` returns every candidate's gender, date of birth, city, marital status and mother tongue to the platform each time the "ForKhatri in numbers" page loads (per-request, not stored, platform key). The owner decided (2026-09-27) that the aggregate dashboard is open to all members, and only counts leave. The issue is not the aggregate but the notice (a matrimonial profile was created for matching, not statistics) and small cells (a handful of women aged 25-29 in a small town can be identified). I did not check whether a minimum count is enforced.
Recommendation: say so in the Mangalya notice, send age bands instead of dates of birth from Mangalya, and suppress any cell below about 10 people. Effort S.

**H6. Volunteer moderation cannot meet the deadlines the product promises.**
Evidence: Mangalya's tiers promise human response in 12-36 hours and paging for the high tier; the IT Rules amendment of 10 Feb 2026 (effective 20 Feb 2026) cut takedown on government or court order to 3 hours, intimate-image removal to 2 hours, general grievance resolution to 7 days (https://www.argus-p.com/updates/updates/update-overview-of-the-it-intermediary-amendment-rules-2026/). Research on volunteer moderators finds burnout and conflict as the main reason they quit (New Media & Society 2022, https://doi.org/10.1177/14614448221138529; Cao et al., arXiv 2311.07879). Samaj heads change every year.
Recommendation: pay for a small, trained trust-and-safety rota (even two part-time people) and keep volunteers for appeals and community context; make appeals go to someone who did not decide (Santa Clara Principles, https://santaclaraprinciples.org/); publish quarterly counts. Effort M.

### Medium

**M1. "Keeps nothing" versus the evidence rules.** Mangalya help promises a candidate chat that "keeps nothing"; the system also scans messages at send time, keeps a safety-evidence exception for 180 days after a case, and DPDP Rule 8(3) requires retaining logs for a year. Not necessarily in conflict, but the wording must say exactly what is and is not kept, or it risks an unfair-trade-practice complaint. Effort S.

**M2. Phone number as the only anchor.** Phone reuse and SIM-swap can hand a matrimonial account to a new number holder; there is no device list or step-up for sensitive changes (unverified how long operators wait before reassigning numbers **[U]**). Add re-verification before showing contact or exporting data, a visible "your devices" list, and passkeys later. Effort M.

**M3. Parent-created adult profiles.** A parent can provision a member for an adult child by phone (`profile.py`); the child "owns" it on first OTP. Consent of the data principal is retrospective at best; DPDP recognises a guardian only for children and for persons with disabilities needing one (PIB, 17 Nov 2025). Create the profile hidden, tell the person by SMS, and make it invisible until they confirm. Effort S.

**M4. Counsel's professional regulation.** Register checks (Bar Council, ICAI, NMC, SEBI) are blocked (backlog B3). BCI told platforms to remove lawyer ads in July 2024 and issued digital conduct rules reported on 18 Jul 2026, with a Supreme Court notice on advocate advertising on 14-15 Jul 2026 (Google News listings, outlet and date confirmed, article link unresolved **[U]**). Ratings and rankings of lawyers may count as advertising. Launch with lawyers, doctors and financial advice switched off until registers are checked; keep life-skills and career advice open. Effort S.

**M5. Reviews, ranking and dark patterns.** The E-Commerce Rules require ranking explanations, a grievance officer who acknowledges in 48 hours and resolves in a month, and ban fake reviews (https://www.indialaw.in/blog/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules/); the CCPA dark-patterns guidelines list 13 patterns (30 Nov 2023). Vyapar separates promoted from organic results, which is right; add a short "why this ranks here" and an audit of paid-plan flows in Mangalya agents and Counsel. Effort S.

**M6. Aadhaar and ID.** Puttaswamy (26 Sep 2018) bars private bodies from demanding Aadhaar by contract; do not make it a verification requirement; DigiLocker or offline routes need legal review **[U]**.

## 3. New in the world

1. DPDP Rules 2025 timeline and a possible 12-month cut for large fiduciaries (sources above).
2. IT Rules amendment 2026: 3-hour and 2-hour takedowns, synthetic-media labelling (Argus, link above).
3. Courts accepting "documented due diligence" as safe harbour for matrimonial portals (Allahabad HC, Sep 2025).
4. StopNCII on-device hashing for intimate images (link above).
5. Tea app breach, Jul 2025: about 72,000 images including ID selfies and 1.1 million private messages exposed; Apple removed the app (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_(app)). The lesson: do not keep ID and selfie files, which Mangalya's design already avoids.
6. Volunteer moderation research on tooling gaps (Bajpai and Chandrasekharan, arXiv 2409.16840).
7. Caste-filter debate in matrimony (Indian Express, 14 Jul 2026, Google News listing **[U]** link); an earlier arXiv study of 313K profiles (https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.04176).

## 4. Rethink

1. **"Private by default, one account everywhere."** For: simple, trusted. Against: a single phone-linked identity across matrimony, legal advice and jobs raises the damage of one breach or one nosy office bearer. Consider unlinkable per-module pseudonyms for Counsel and Mangalya.
2. **"Samaj heads run it."** For: community trust and local knowledge. Against: personal liability (s.85), conflicts of interest, annual turnover, and nobody trained for 2-hour takedowns. Consider a professional operator with samaj oversight.
3. **"We do not store faces or IDs."** For: shrinks breach risk. Against: it leaves verification forgeable. A reviewed-then-discarded video check keeps both benefits.
4. **"Community-bound means no discrimination issue."** For: the app is openly for Khatris. Against: sub-caste, gotra and "looking for" filters add exclusion layers; the DPDP Act has no "sensitive data" class so caste gets no extra legal shield, and public opinion is moving. Keep filters opt-in, never default.
5. **"Compliance can wait for the rules to start."** For: obligations begin about May 2027. Against: retrofitting consent, deletion and age gating across four modules costs far more than building it now, and the Rules' 90-day clock begins on day one.

## 5. Do not change

- Platform keeps basic identity only; module data stays in module databases with row-level security.
- Opaque HttpOnly session cookies, digests rather than secrets, Argon2id, rate limits, no account enumeration (08-security-performance.md).
- Maker-checker approvals, role terms, append-only audit trigger, contact reveals audited (migration 005).
- Dev "act as" tools are mounted only in development and refused by the production guard (`settings.py`).
- Mangalya's two-party consent for sharing contact, Home Circle boundaries, no scanning of photos or video, human review for reports, and DEC-V1-016's refusal to store biometrics.
- Counsel's encrypted files, 90/365-day file retention, refuse-delete while money is owed, payout details encrypted.
- Vyapar's six plain privacy controls, learned-taste switch-off that clears what was learned, and promoted-versus-organic labelling.
- Mangalya "Who sees what" panel, in plain words, on the profile screen.
- Milavn's venue privacy (QA run 2026-10-06: about 30 endpoints leaked nothing to non-attendees).
